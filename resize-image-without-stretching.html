How to Resize an Image Without Stretching It

Keep an image from stretching by preserving its aspect ratio: change width and height proportionally. If the destination has a different shape, choose cropping or fit-with-space instead of forcing the image into new proportions.

Why images look stretched

An image's aspect ratio is its width compared with its height. A 1600 × 900 image is 16:9. Forcing it into 1000 × 1000 without cropping makes objects look wider or taller than they should.

  • Preserve proportions: scale both dimensions by the same factor so the whole image stays visible.
  • Crop to fill: fill a differently shaped frame by trimming edges.
  • Fit with space: show the whole image and leave blank or background space around it.

How to resize without distortion

  1. Choose your imageOpen the image resizer and select a JPG, PNG, or WebP file.
  2. Keep the ratio lockedChanging width should adjust height proportionally. If you need a different shape, use crop or fit instead of forcing both numbers.
  3. Set target dimensionsUse the pixel dimensions required by the destination. Upscaling cannot restore detail missing from the source.
  4. Preview and downloadCheck faces, circles, logos and text for distortion, then confirm the output dimensions and format.

Example: resize 1600 × 900

If the width becomes 800 pixels and the ratio is preserved, height becomes 450 pixels: 800 × 900 ÷ 1600 = 450. The output is 800 × 450, not 800 × 800.

For a square destination, crop the sides or fit the whole image inside a square with space around it. Choose based on whether keeping every edge or filling the frame matters more.

Common mistakes

  • Unlocking width and height: re-enable the ratio lock or use crop/fit.
  • Enlarging a small source: use a higher-resolution original when possible.
  • Cropping important content: preview and reposition the crop.
  • Confusing pixels with file size: dimensions describe pixel width and height; encoding affects file size.

Image resizing FAQ

What is an aspect ratio?

It is the relationship between width and height, commonly written as 16:9 or 1:1. Keeping it constant prevents geometric distortion.

Can I make an image square without cropping?

You can fit the whole image inside a square and leave space, but filling a square without distortion generally requires cropping or a background.
